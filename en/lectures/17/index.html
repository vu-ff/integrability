Lecture 17

The quantum inverse scattering method

What this lecture does

This is Lecture 8, quantised. The Sklyanin bracket becomes the RTTRTTRTT relation. The transfer matrix becomes an operator that commutes with itself at every value of the spectral parameter. At one special value it is the lattice shift, and the Heisenberg chain’s Hamiltonian falls out of its logarithmic derivative, followed by a tower of local charges. Then the lecture restores ℏ\hbarℏ in the quantum periodic Toda chain and recovers the r\rmatr-matrix of Lecture 10 as ℏ→0\hbar\to0ℏ→0, sign for sign.

17.1 From brackets to commutators

Lecture 8 built commuting integrals from one structure. A chain of NNN sites has a Lax matrix L(n)(λ)L^{(n)}(\lambda)L(n)(λ) at each site, and the site matrices at different sites Poisson-commute (the chain is ultralocal). If each obeys the quadratic bracket {L1(λ),L2(μ)}=[r12(λ−μ),L1(λ)L2(μ)], \pb{\leg{L}{1}(\lambda)}{\leg{L}{2}(\mu)} =\comm{\leg{\rmat}{12}(\lambda-\mu)}{\leg{L}{1}(\lambda)\leg{L}{2}(\mu)}, {L1​(λ),L2​(μ)}=[r12​(λ−μ),L1​(λ)L2​(μ)],(17.1) then so does the monodromy matrix T=L(N)⋯L(1)\mono=L^{(N)}\cdots L^{(1)}T=L(N)⋯L(1) (Theorem 8.17), and the transfer matrices t=tr⁡T\transfer=\tr\monot=trT Poisson-commute for all λ,μ\lambda,\muλ,μ (Corollary 8.18). The periodic Toda chain is the example: its site matrix (10.8) satisfies (17.1) with r(λ)=−P/λ\rmat(\lambda)=-\perm/\lambdar(λ)=−P/λ (Proposition 10.4).

Quantisation makes the entries of LLL operators and brackets commutators, {⋅,⋅}→[⋅,⋅]/iℏ\pb{\cdot}{\cdot}\to\comm{\cdot}{\cdot}/\ii\hbar{⋅,⋅}→[⋅,⋅]/iℏ (13.1); the legs of Definition 8.1 survive. §8.9 found the quantum relation that returns (17.1) at order ℏ\hbarℏ: R12(λ−μ) T2(μ) T1(λ)=T1(λ) T2(μ) R12(λ−μ),R=1+iℏr+O(ℏ2), \Rmat_{12}(\lambda-\mu)\,\leg{\mono}{2}(\mu)\,\leg{\mono}{1}(\lambda) =\leg{\mono}{1}(\lambda)\,\leg{\mono}{2}(\mu)\,\Rmat_{12}(\lambda-\mu), \qquad \Rmat=\Id+\ii\hbar\rmat+O(\hbar^{2}), R12​(λ−μ)T2​(μ)T1​(λ)=T1​(λ)T2​(μ)R12​(λ−μ),R=1+iℏr+O(ℏ2),(17.2) which is (8.17). The order of the two monodromies is not a choice. With the +++ in R=1+iℏr\Rmat=\Id+\ii\hbar\rmatR=1+iℏr fixed by (8.16), the other order returns the bracket with r→−r\rmat\to-\rmatr→−r (Remark 8.19); §17.6 takes the limit properly. From here to §17.6, ℏ=1\hbar=1ℏ=1. The auxiliary copies of C2\C^{2}C2 get letters, aaa and bbb, to keep them apart from the sites n=1,…,Nn=1,\dots,Nn=1,…,N of the chain. The lecture constructs criterion (T) of §13.5 in full for one model.

What RTTRTTRTT gives, and what it does not

The proof used (17.7) and the invertibility of R\RmatR, and not the Yang–Baxter equation. The equation does two other jobs. It supplies Lax operators: by (17.8) the rational R\RmatR, read with a site as its second space, is a Lax operator for its own RLLRLLRLL relation, which is why the Heisenberg chain has one. (The step used R12=R21\Rmat_{12}=\Rmat_{21}R12​=R21​; for a solution without that symmetry the recipe is Lan(λ)=−Rna(−λ)\Lop{an}(\lambda)=-\Rmat_{na}(-\lambda)Lan​(λ)=−Rna​(−λ).) And it is sufficient, not forced, for the consistency of the RTTRTTRTT relations when they are taken to define an algebra: reordering three monodromies along the two routes of Figure 17.5 requires a combination of R\RmatR’s only to commute with the triple products — Remark 8.13 quantised, proved in the reading at the end. This is Lecture 0’s “sufficient, not forced”. For the Heisenberg chain nothing is left to choose: the check script finds that (17.5) fixes R\RmatR up to a scalar for one and two sites, at generic λ,μ\lambda,\muλ,μ.

Finally, integrability does not require RTTRTTRTT: the free-fermion charges of Lecture 14 were constructed directly, and the Calogero–Sutherland charges of Lecture 21 will be too. Nor does RTTRTTRTT decide completeness, which remains Lecture 15’s cited question.

17.11 Why the zero-curvature equation is not what is quantised

Lecture 10 derived the conservation of t(λ)\transfer(\lambda)t(λ) from the zero-curvature equation (10.10), L˙n=Vn+1Ln−LnVn\dot L_n=V_{n+1}L_n-L_nV_nL˙n​=Vn+1​Ln​−Ln​Vn​, and then, separately, the Sklyanin bracket. This lecture quantised only the second. The zero-curvature equation is the equation of motion under one Hamiltonian, and in quantum mechanics equations of motion are not postulated: they follow from [⋅,H]\comm{\cdot}{H}[⋅,H] once HHH is known. What has to be quantised is the kinematics, the brackets, and from them the commuting family and its Hamiltonians follow. The site-by-site transport survives as the monodromy (17.6), and the bracket as RTTRTTRTT. The Heisenberg equation for T(λ)\mono(\lambda)T(λ) under HHH is then a consequence, and [H,t(λ)]=0\comm{H}{\transfer(\lambda)}=0[H,t(λ)]=0 is Theorem 17.4, not an input. That is the promise of Lecture 10, and the reason the classical limit of §17.6 was taken in the bracket, not in the equation of motion.
