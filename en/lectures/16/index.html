Lecture 16

Coordinate Bethe ansatz II: Lieb–Liniger and Yang–Baxter

What this lecture does

The method of Lecture 15 works for particles on a line. Bosons with a contact interaction collide pairwise, each collision multiplies the wave by a phase, and the Bethe equations follow as before — this time with a proof that the wavefunction is an eigenfunction. Then comes the question the whole algebraic method rests on. If the particles carry a label, such as a spin, a collision is a matrix, and three-body scattering can factorise into two-body collisions only if the two orders of Figure 5 give the same matrix. That condition is the Yang–Baxter equation, and here it is derived, not postulated.

Rapidities. On a ring the particles never become free, yet every eigenstate carries a set {kj}\{k_j\}{kj​}, the rapidities, which enter EEE and KKK as if they were: the quantum counterpart of the asymptotic momenta of Theorem 9.9. They are conserved in the sense that matters: the Bethe states form an orthogonal basis (Dorlas 1993) cited, so in any state the probability of each set {kj}\{k_j\}{kj​} is the weight of one eigenstate, constant in time. That conserved distribution is what §0.2 said would stop the cradle’s gas relaxing; Lecture 23 builds on it.

16.4 Why one dimension

§0.2 counted. For two equal masses, momentum and energy are two equations for two outgoing momenta, solved only by the incoming pair or its exchange (Proposition 0.1). Quantum mechanically the same holds for any short-range pair interaction, once the particles are free again: the outgoing wave of two particles contains only the momenta k1,k2k_1,k_2k1​,k2​, and scattering fixes nothing but amplitudes.

For three particles the count fails: two equations for three unknowns leave a circle of outgoing momenta, the six rearrangements being six points on it. A generic three-body collision sends waves out over the whole circle — diffraction — which is how a one-dimensional gas can thermalise (Sutherland, §2.2). Non-diffraction is not free. It is what the Bethe wavefunction asserts — in (16.8) only rearrangements of the incoming momenta ever appear — and Theorem 16.3 proves that the delta gas has it. One caution: the two-body count uses E=k2E=k^{2}E=k2; on a lattice even two magnons are not forced by kinematics (§15.3, at K=πK=\piK=π).

In Figure 16.5 each crossing multiplies the amplitude by one SSS. Had the lines crossed in the other order — the other panel of Figure 5 — the same factors would come in another order, and nothing would change: for bosons without labels three-body consistency is automatic. It stops being automatic when a collision can do more than multiply by a number.

Summary

  • The gas: H=−∑∂j2+2c∑δ(xi−xj)H=-\sum\partial_j^{2}+2c\sum\delta(x_i-x_j)H=−∑∂j2​+2c∑δ(xi​−xj​), c>0c>0c>0, ℏ=2m=1\hbar=2m=1ℏ=2m=1. The delta is a contact condition, (∂2−∂1−c)ψ=0(\partial_2-\partial_1-c)\psi=0(∂2​−∂1​−c)ψ=0 on the diagonal (16.3).
  • Two bosons: A21/A12=S(k1−k2)A_{21}/A_{12}=S(k_1-k_2)A21​/A12​=S(k1​−k2​), S(k)=(k−ic)/(k+ic)=−eiθ(k)S(k)=(k-\ii c)/(k+\ii c)=-e^{\ii\theta(k)}S(k)=(k−ic)/(k+ic)=−eiθ(k) (Proposition 16.1); a wave packet comes out displaced by ∓θ′(k)\mp\theta'(k)∓θ′(k), the counterpart of the Toda displacement (an analogy; for the Toda pair itself the reading gives the ℏ→0\hbar\to0ℏ→0 limit).
  • NNN bosons: the Bethe wavefunction is an eigenfunction under eikjL=∏l≠j(kj−kl+ic)/(kj−kl−ic)e^{\ii k_jL}=\prod_{l\ne j}(k_j-k_l+\ii c)/(k_j-k_l-\ii c)eikj​L=∏l=j​(kj​−kl​+ic)/(kj​−kl​−ic), with E=∑kj2E=\sum k_j^{2}E=∑kj2​ (Theorem 16.3, proved). The roots are real (Proposition 16.4), and each set of distinct IjI_jIj​ gives exactly one solution (Proposition 16.5).
  • One dimension: two-body scattering can only permute momenta; three-body scattering can diffract, and non-diffraction is what the Bethe wavefunction asserts.
  • Labels: A′=Y(k1−k2)AA'=Y(k_1-k_2)AA′=Y(k1​−k2​)A, YB=(kP−ic)/(k+ic)Y_{\mathrm B}=(kP-\ii c)/(k+\ii c)YB​=(kP−ic)/(k+ic), YF=−(kP+ic)/(k+ic)Y_{\mathrm F}=-(kP+\ii c)/(k+\ii c)YF​=−(kP+ic)/(k+ic) (Proposition 16.6); in the momentum basis X=PYX=PYX=PY, XB=R(u)/(u+i)X_{\mathrm B}=\Rmat(u)/(u+\ii)XB​=R(u)/(u+i) with R(u)=u−iP\Rmat(u)=u-\ii PR(u)=u−iP.
  • Yang–Baxter: a consistent factorised three-body ansatz requires X12X13X23=X23X13X12X_{12}X_{13}X_{23}=X_{23}X_{13}X_{12}X12​X13​X23​=X23​X13​X12​ (Theorem 16.8); Yang’s matrix satisfies it because u13=u12+u23u_{13}=u_{12}+u_{23}u13​=u12​+u23​ (Proposition 16.9); for a scalar SSS it is automatic.
