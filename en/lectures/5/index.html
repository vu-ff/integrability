Lecture 5

The Liouville–Arnold theorem

What this lecture does

Course map: G1, step 5 of 9. The central theorem of Part I. On a 2n2n2n-dimensional phase space, take nnn integrals in involution. On a compact connected component of one of their common level sets, where they are independent at every point, the motion lies on an nnn-dimensional torus and is a straight line on it, traversed at constant speed. We state the theorem with its hypotheses visible, prove it in six stages, and say at which stage each hypothesis is spent. Without compactness, but with complete flows, the same stages give a cylinder Tk×Rn−kT^{k}\times\R^{n-k}Tk×Rn−k instead, which is the case the Toda ring of Lecture 9 needs.

5.1 The question

Functions in involution have commuting Hamiltonian vector fields, whose flows commute where they are complete (Corollary 2.6), and at most nnn independent functions can be in involution (Proposition 4.11). A system with nnn of them has as many conserved quantities as it can have. So the question is

what do nnn independent integrals in involution force the motion to be?

The answer, Theorem 5.7, concerns a connected component of a common level set on which the integrals are independent at every point. If that component is compact, the motion is a straight line on a torus; if it is not, but the flows on it are complete, it is a straight line on a cylinder. The worked example is the two-dimensional oscillator (Example 5.3), whose level sets with both energies positive are the tori of Example 4.15.

5.2 Independence and involution

A first integral of HHH is a function FFF with {F,H}=0\pb{F}{H}=0{F,H}=0 (Definition 2.3). 5.4 The theorem, hypothesis by hypothesis

Theorem 5.7 (Liouville–Arnold proved modulo Lemma 5.10 and Theorem 1.9). Let (M,ω)(\phase,\symp)(M,ω) be a symplectic manifold of dimension 2n2n2n, and F1,…,FnF_1,\dots,F_nF1​,…,Fn​ functions in involution. Fix f∈Rnf\in\R^{n}f∈Rn, let Σ\SigmaΣ be a connected component of the level set Mf\phase_fMf​, and suppose

  1. (I)

    dF1,…,dFn\dd F_1,\dots,\dd F_ndF1​,…,dFn​ are linearly independent at every point of Σ\SigmaΣ; and

  2. (C)

    Σ\SigmaΣ is compact.

  2. Σ\SigmaΣ is diffeomorphic to the torus Tn=Rn/2πZnT^{n}=\R^{n}/2\pi\Z^{n}Tn=Rn/2πZn, with angle coordinates φ1,…,φn\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_nφ1​,…,φn​ defined modulo 2π2\pi2π;

Deleting any one hypothesis gives a counterexample; three are exercises. The one to see now is compactness, the one the course will not have. The free particle H=12p2H=\tfrac12p^{2}H=21​p2 on R2\R^{2}R2 is integrable, with n=1n=1n=1. At E>0E>0E>0 each component of its level set is a line p=±2Ep=\pm\sqrt{2E}p=±2E​, and the motion on it is not periodic: this is T0×R1T^{0}\times\R^{1}T0×R1. When compactness fails but the flows on Σ\SigmaΣ are complete, stage (iv) of the proof shows that a cylinder of this kind is the shape of the conclusion; without completeness there is no such classification. The Toda ring of Lecture 9 is the case the course needs it for.

5.5 The proof, in six stages

5.6 What it buys

  • Tori, or cylinders. If the flows are complete on Σ\SigmaΣ — which compactness guarantees but need not be what supplies it — stages (i)–(iv) give Σ≅Tk×Rn−k\Sigma\cong T^{k}\times\R^{n-k}Σ≅Tk×Rn−k, and compactness is needed only to force k=nk=nk=n. For the Toda ring, whose level sets are not compact, Lecture 9 proves completeness separately and finds TN−1×RT^{N-1}\times\RTN−1×R.

  • Angles and cycles. The period lattice gives angle coordinates, nnn basis cycles and a frequency vector (5.9); and, in R2n\R^{2n}R2n or a cotangent bundle, p⋅dqp\cdot\dd qp⋅dq is closed on the torus (Proposition 5.12). Lecture 6 integrates it over the cycles to get the actions, and completes the angles to canonical coordinates.

5.7 Caveats, in one place

  • Status. Theorem 5.7 is proved modulo the cited Lemma 5.10 and Theorem 1.9. Stage (ii) also uses Corollary 2.6 on M\phaseM. On R2n\R^{2n}R2n and on cotangent bundles, where every cotangent chart is canonical (Theorem 4.17), the coordinate proof of Lecture 2 gives it. On a general symplectic manifold it rests on the Jacobi identity for the bracket of ω\sympω and the second proof of the engine, both proved in readings (Lecture 4, Lecture 2).

  • Independent on a dense set, or on all of Σ\SigmaΣ. The definition allows the integrals to be dependent somewhere; the oscillator’s are, on two planes. The theorem applies only to the components of level sets on which they are independent at every point.

  • One torus at a time. The theorem is about one level set. How the tori fit together as fff varies is Lecture 6’s business near one torus; globally it can be subtle (optional lecture A).

Summary

  • The definition is algebraic: nnn independent functions in involution on a 2n2n2n-dimensional phase space, with HHH a function of them (Definition 5.5).
  • The theorem (Theorem 5.7): a compact connected component of a level set, on which the differentials are independent, is a Lagrangian torus TnT^{n}Tn, and the motion on it is φ(t)=φ(0)+νt\varphi(t)=\varphi(0)+\freq tφ(t)=φ(0)+νt.
  • The proof: tangent commuting flows, complete by compactness, give an action of Rn\R^{n}Rn; independence and connectedness make it transitive, with a discrete stabiliser Λ\LambdaΛ; the quotient is Tk×Rn−kT^{k}\times\R^{n-k}Tk×Rn−k, and compactness forces k=nk=nk=n.
  • The period lattice Λ\LambdaΛ gives angles, basis cycles and frequencies (5.9); in R2n\R^{2n}R2n or a cotangent bundle, p⋅dqp\cdot\dd qp⋅dq is closed on the torus (Proposition 5.12).
  • Next: Lecture 6 integrates p⋅dqp\cdot\dd qp⋅dq over the basis cycles, and finds canonical action–angle coordinates near the torus.
