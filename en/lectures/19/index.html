Lecture 19

Why integrable systems do not thermalise

What this lecture does

Course map: P5, and the first half of G4. Lecture 0 began with two experiments that refused to come to equilibrium and promised that the reason was integrability. An isolated quantum system evolves unitarily, so nothing relaxes in the strict sense; what can relax is a local observable, and whether it relaxes to a thermal value depends on what the conserved charges remember. A generic chain is expected to remember only its energy and its symmetry charges; an integrable one remembers much more, and where it relaxes, the expected answer is the maximum-entropy state compatible with a complete family of its conserved charges, the generalised Gibbs ensemble. For the magnetisation after a field quench of Lecture 18’s Ising chain this is a theorem.

19.1 The question

Lecture 0’s cradle did not thermalise, and Lecture 18 solved a chain with a conserved occupation for every mode. So the question is

if an isolated quantum system evolves unitarily, what relaxes, and to what?

The answer: local observables, when they relax at all, to the average in the ensemble fixed by a complete family of conserved charges (Definition 19.5); for the magnetisation after a field quench of the Ising chain this is proved (Theorem 19.7), and the Gibbs ensemble gets it wrong (Figure 19.2). The worked example is that quench.

19.2 Local and extensive charges

Densities

From here on the system is a ring of NNN sites, each carrying a spin 12\tfrac1221​: the Hilbert space is (C2)⊗N(\C^{2})^{\otimes N}(C2)⊗N, site N+1N+1N+1 is site 111, and τ\tauτ is the translation that moves every site one step, n↦n+1n\mapsto n+1n↦n+1. Definition 19.1 (Local charge). A density of range rrr is an operator qqq acting on sites 1,…,r1,\dots,r1,…,r. On a ring of N≥rN\ge rN≥r sites its local charge is the sum of its translates, Q=∑n=1Nτ n−1(q),Q=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau^{\,n-1}(q),Q=n=1∑N​τn−1(q), τ n−1(q)\tau^{\,n-1}(q)τn−1(q) acting on sites n,…,n+r−1n,\dots,n+r-1n,…,n+r−1. A local charge that commutes with HHH is a local conserved charge.

The fixed range is the whole content: with r=Nr=Nr=N allowed, any translation-invariant operator AAA would qualify, as the sum of the translates of A/NA/NA/N. The total spin Sz=∑nSnzS^{z}=\sum_n S^{z}_nSz=∑n​Snz​ is a local charge of range 111, and so is a nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian such as Lecture 18’s Ising chain, of range 222. Local charges are extensive: QQQ is a sum of NNN terms of norm ∥q∥\norm{q}∥q∥, so ∥Q∥≤N∥q∥\norm{Q}\le N\norm{q}∥Q∥≤N∥q∥, and in a translation-invariant state ⟨Q⟩=N⟨q⟩\langle Q\rangle=N\langle q\rangle⟨Q⟩=N⟨q⟩ — proportional to the length, as energy and particle number are. And local charges are rare.

Proposition 19.2 (Local charges are few). For each fixed rrr, the local charges of range at most rrr on a ring of N≥rN\ge rN≥r sites form a vector space of dimension at most 4r4^{r}4r, whatever NNN. So among the 2N2^{N}2N linearly independent conserved operators of a Hamiltonian with simple spectrum, the local conserved charges of range at most rrr span at most 4r4^{r}4r dimensions.

Proof. A density of range rrr is an operator on (C2)⊗r(\C^{2})^{\otimes r}(C2)⊗r, a space of dimension 4r4^{r}4r, and q↦Qq\mapsto Qq↦Q is linear. A density of smaller range is one of range rrr that acts trivially on the last sites.

The hypothesis that makes a generic system thermal

Definition 19.4 (Eigenstate thermalisation cited: Deutsch 1991, Srednicki 1994). A Hamiltonian satisfies the eigenstate thermalisation hypothesis (ETH) for a local observable O\mathcal OO if, within each sector of fixed symmetry charges (such as SzS^{z}Sz and momentum) and in the bulk of its spectrum, PE O PE=[Omc(E/N)+a fluctuation vanishing as N→∞] PE, P_E\,\mathcal O\,P_E=\Big[\mathcal O_{\mathrm{mc}}(E/N) +\text{a fluctuation vanishing as }N\to\infty\Big]\,P_E , PE​OPE​=[Omc​(E/N)+a fluctuation vanishing as N→∞]PE​,(19.3) with PEP_EPE​ the projector on energy EEE within the sector and Omc\mathcal O_{\mathrm{mc}}Omc​ a smooth function — the microcanonical average in that sector at that energy density. If (19.3) holds and ∣ψ0⟩\ket{\psi_0}∣ψ0​⟩ lies in one symmetry sector with subextensive energy fluctuations, Proposition 19.3 averages a smooth function over a narrow window and the answer is the microcanonical one: the system thermalises, in the only sense a closed quantum system allows. Nothing has been proved — ETH is a hypothesis about a Hamiltonian, checked numerically model by model, and (19.3), sector by sector, is exactly the statement a figure can test (Figure 19.1).

The charges are the memory

The left panel is not an accident of the observable. A chain with about NNN local conserved charges, all commuting, can have its eigenstates labelled by all of them at once. What is expected to replace (19.3) there is a generalised eigenstate thermalisation: ⟨n∣O∣n⟩\bra n\mathcal O\ket n⟨n∣O∣n⟩ a smooth function of the densities of a complete family of conserved charges — the strictly local ones for the Ising chain, quasi-local ones as well for the XXZ chain (Lecture 20’s reading) cited: Caux and Essler 2013. That reduces to a function of the energy alone only if the other densities are fixed by it, and they are not: two eigenstates at the same energy density generically carry different values of the other charges. The charges remember which state the system was prepared in, and a local observable can see the memory. The example this lecture proves is the Ising chain of Lecture 18, whose local charges are those of Proposition 19.8.

19.5 What it buys

  • Lecture 0’s question, answered in one model: after a quench the Ising chain’s magnetisation relaxes, and to the generalised Gibbs value, not the Gibbs one (Theorem 19.7, Figure 19.2).

  • Why: the occupations nkn_knk​ are conserved (Proposition 19.6), and the local charges fix them (Proposition 19.8); a local observable remembers them.

  • The ensemble that Lecture 20 carries to the interacting gas, where one function, the rapidity density, plays the part of the nkn_knk​.

19.6 Caveats, in one place

  • Status. Proposition 19.3 (proof in a reading), Proposition 19.6 and Theorem 19.7 are proved; Proposition 19.8 is sketched. ETH is a hypothesis, checked numerically; generalised ETH is cited; the GGE’s definition is cited.

  • The hypotheses of Theorem 19.7 are spent. At h0=Jh_0=Jh0​=J the decay is slower, at h=0h=0h=0 it does not happen, at h0=0h_0=0h0​=0 there is no single ground state, and at h=Jh=Jh=J the observable mzm^{z}mz cannot tell the Gibbs and generalised ensembles apart.

  • The order of the limits: N→∞N\to\inftyN→∞ first, then t→∞t\to\inftyt→∞. At finite NNN the magnetisation revives, and only its time average converges.

  • Which charges. For the Ising chain the strictly local charges fix the occupations almost everywhere (Proposition 19.8); for the XXZ chain the ultralocal charges are not enough, and quasi-local ones are needed (Lecture 20’s reading).

  • Every statement about a non-integrable chain is numerical, and a state that carries a thermal state’s charge densities relaxes to thermal values even in an integrable chain.

Reading

19.7 This was known in 1970

Remark 19.9 (This was known in 1970). Barouch, McCoy and Dresden computed the magnetisation of the XY chain after a field quench in 1970 and found it non-ergodic: it does not approach the equilibrium value. The ensemble that explains it was written down thirty-seven years later.

Summary

  • Local charges (Definition 19.1): fixed range, extensive, and few (Proposition 19.2).
  • Dephasing (Proposition 19.3): a local observable’s time average is fixed by the energy-dephased state (the diagonal ensemble when the spectrum is non-degenerate); ETH (Definition 19.4), sector by sector, makes it thermal in a generic chain.
  • The GGE (Definition 19.5): maximum entropy given all the charges.
  • The Ising quench (Proposition 19.6 and Theorem 19.7): the occupations are conserved, the magnetisation relaxes like t−3/2t^{-3/2}t−3/2 to the GGE value, and the Gibbs value at the same energy is different.
