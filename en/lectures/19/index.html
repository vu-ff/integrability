Lecture 19

The thermodynamic limit: densities, ground states, excitations

What this lecture does

Until now a Bethe state has been a finite list of roots. Let N→∞N\to\inftyN→∞ at fixed filling and the list becomes a density, the Bethe equations one linear integral equation for it. Solving that equation is this lecture: by Fourier transform it gives the Heisenberg antiferromagnet’s ground-state energy −Jln⁡2-J\ln2−Jln2 exactly, and numerically it gives the Lieb–Liniger gas its equation of state from weak coupling to the impenetrable limit, where it turns back into free fermions. Removing one root from the sea leaves two holes of spin 12\tfrac1221​: the spinons, whose continuum neutron scattering sees. Densities, dressing and the dressed velocity are what Lecture 20 runs at finite temperature and Lecture 23 out of equilibrium.

19.4 Dressing
