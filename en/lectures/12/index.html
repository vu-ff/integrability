Lecture 12

Hamiltonian reduction: where the Lax pair comes from

What this lecture does

Moser’s matrix was a rabbit. Here it stops being one. A free particle moving in a straight line through the N×NN\times NN×N Hermitian matrices has eigenvalues that repel — and are Calogero–Moser particles. The machinery that says why is built only for this example: conjugation, its momentum map (a commutator), a level set, a gauge fixing. Reducing at momentum zero gives free particles, the wrong model; at one non-zero value it gives Calogero–Moser, with Moser’s LLL the gauge-fixed momentum and Moser’s MMM the velocity of the gauge transformation.

12.1 Free motion in disguise

Start with the picture (Figure 12.1). Take two 3×33\times33×3 Hermitian matrices X0X_0X0​, P0P_0P0​ and move along the straight line X(t)=X0+t P0. X(t)=X_0+t\,P_0 .
