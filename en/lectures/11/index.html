Lecture 11

Calogero–Moser: the model, its Lax pair and its scattering

What this lecture does

Every particle now interacts with every other, through an inverse-square potential or one of its three relatives. We derive Moser’s Lax pair for three of the four from one functional identity (the fourth, elliptic, solves the same identity, cited); prove the integrals independent; and find that involution needs an r\rmatr-matrix of a kind Lecture 8 only warned about. It depends on the positions and is not skew, and for three particles no r\rmatr at all puts the bracket in Lecture 8’s single-commutator form. The involution theorem survives anyway. The rational gas scatters like the Toda chain, momenta permuted, but with no displacement whatever; in a harmonic trap every orbit is periodic.

So the machinery did not fail at its first real test. What Lecture 8 proved was more than it assumed: the involution theorem needs only that the bracket is a sum of commutators with L1\leg{L}{1}L1​ and with L2\leg{L}{2}L2​, and Babelon–Viallet (Lecture 8, reading) says that some such form always exists behind involution. A constant, skew r\rmatr is the exception — the open Toda chain has one — and a dynamical r\rmatr the general situation, as Babelon and Viallet describe it. What does change is Jacobi: Theorem 8.11 was proved for a numerical skew r\rmatr. For their elliptic dynamical r\rmatr, Babelon–Bernard–Talon show by direct calculation a generalised Yang–Baxter equation (7.21), with new terms that are Poisson brackets of LLL with r\rmatr, and that it implies Jacobi cited; in general Jacobi gives a weaker condition, and r\rmatr has gauge freedom. Here Jacobi is not in question: the bracket is the canonical one.

11.5 Scattering, and the comparison with Toda

In the rational model the particles repel and the potential dies away, so, as in §9.7, they separate (Moser). Once they have, L→diag⁡(p)L\to\operatorname{diag}(p)L→diag(p), and the one-line argument after Theorem 9.9 applies word for word: the asymptotic momenta are the eigenvalues λ1<⋯<λN\lambda_1<\dots<\lambda_Nλ1​<⋯<λN​ of LLL, arriving in decreasing order along the line and leaving in increasing order (Figure 11.5). The scattering is elastic and non-diffractive.

The positions are where Calogero–Moser and Toda part company.

Summary

  • The model (11.1): pair potentials 1/x21/x^{2}1/x2, 1/sinh⁡2x1/\sinh^{2}x1/sinh2x (scattering), 1/sin⁡2x1/\sin^{2}x1/sin2x, ℘(x)\wp(x)℘(x) (on a circle, confined).
  • Moser’s Lax pair, derived (Theorem 11.1): within a pairwise ansatz, tr⁡L2=2H\tr L^{2}=2HtrL2=2H gives V=x2V=x^{2}V=x2, the momentum terms give Mjk=−ig x′(qjk)M_{jk}=-\ii g\,x'(q_{jk})Mjk​=−igx′(qjk​), and the rest is the functional identity (11.4), which holds for x=1/ux=1/ux=1/u, 1/sin⁡u1/\sin u1/sinu, 1/sinh⁡u1/\sinh u1/sinhu; the elliptic case is cited.
  • Independence (Theorem 11.3) on a dense open set, by the top-degree Vandermonde.
  • The dynamical r\rmatr-matrix (Propositions 11.4 and 11.5): for N=2,3N=2,3N=2,3 no constant r\rmatr, and for N=3N=3N=3 no single-commutator r\rmatr at all; the two-term form holds with an r\rmatr that depends on positions and is not skew. Involution survives (Theorem 11.6).
  • Scattering (Proposition 11.7 and Theorem 11.8): asymptotic momenta are the eigenvalues of LLL, permuted; unlike Toda, no displacement.
