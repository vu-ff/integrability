Lecture 15

Coordinate Bethe ansatz I: the Heisenberg XXX chain

What this lecture does

In 1931 Bethe wrote down eigenstates of the Heisenberg chain by a guess. Start from all spins up; flip one and it moves as a plane wave; flip two and they collide, and the collision does nothing but multiply the wave by a phase. Carry one flipped spin round the ring and the phases must add up: that condition, the Bethe equations, quantises the momenta. It is the Bohr–Sommerfeld condition of Lecture 5 for a genuinely interacting many-body system, and unlike Bohr–Sommerfeld it is exact. The lecture derives it by hand, solves it, checks it against a computer, and says honestly what is known about whether it finds every state.

15.9 Why Bohr–Sommerfeld works, and why the Bethe equations need no correction

Lecture 5 left two things to Part II: the correction n↦n+12n\mapsto n+\tfrac12n↦n+21​, and the reason (5.18) works at all. Both come from the WKB form of a wavefunction in one dimension, ψ(q)≈C∣p(q)∣ eiS(q)/ℏ,S(q)=∫qp dq′,p(q)=2(E−V(q)),\psi(q)\approx\frac{C}{\sqrt{\abs{p(q)}}}\,e^{\ii S(q)/\hbar},\qquad S(q)=\int^{q}p\,\dd q',\qquad p(q)=\sqrt{2(E-V(q))},ψ(q)≈∣p(q)∣​C​eiS(q)/ℏ,S(q)=∫qpdq′,p(q)=2(E−V(q))​, valid where the wavelength 2πℏ/∣p∣2\pi\hbar/\abs p2πℏ/∣p∣ changes little over one wavelength — that is, away from the turning points, where p=0p=0p=0. Along a closed orbit ψ\psiψ must come back to itself, which asks ∮p dq/ℏ∈2πZ\oint p\,\dd q/\hbar\in2\pi\Z∮pdq/ℏ∈2πZ: that is (5.18), and the reason it works is that eiS/ℏe^{\ii S/\hbar}eiS/ℏ is the leading behaviour of the true wavefunction as ℏ→0\hbar\to0ℏ→0. Near a turning point the approximation fails, but there the potential is nearly linear and the exact solution is an Airy function; matching it to the WKB waves on either side (the connection formulas) shows that each reflection at a smooth turning point costs a quarter period, a phase −π/2-\pi/2−π/2 cited: Landau and Lifshitz, Quantum Mechanics, §47. A libration has two turning points, so ∮p dq=2πℏ(n+12).\oint p\,\dd q=2\pi\hbar\big(n+\tfrac12\big).∮pdq=2πℏ(n+21​). For the oscillator, I=E/νI=E/\freqI=E/ν (§5.2) gives E=ℏν(n+12)E=\hbar\freq(n+\tfrac12)E=ℏν(n+21​), the exact spectrum with its zero-point energy. With nnn degrees of freedom, each basis cycle γi\gamma_iγi​ of the torus gets its own count μi\mu_iμi​ of such phases, the Maslov index, and ∮γip⋅dq=2πℏ(ni+μi/4)\oint_{\gamma_i}p\cdot\dd q=2\pi\hbar(n_i+\mu_i/4)∮γi​​p⋅dq=2πℏ(ni​+μi​/4) (Einstein, Brillouin and Keller) cited. A rotation, such as the pendulum over the top, has no turning point and no correction.

The Bethe equations have neither error. A magnon between collisions is not a WKB wave but an exact plane wave: the lattice has no potential, the momentum is conserved, and there is no turning point — motion round a ring is a rotation. The collisions contribute SSS, which is an exact solution of the two-body problem, not a semiclassical phase. So the round trip of Figure 15.3 is exact, and so is the analogy’s M=1M=1M=1 case.

15.10 The joint spectrum, and Hamiltonian monodromy

The reading of Lecture 4 ended with a promise. For the spherical pendulum HHH and the angular momentum JzJ_zJz​ (Lecture 4’s JJJ; in Part II JJJ is the exchange coupling) commute after quantisation, and their joint eigenvalues (E,ℏm)(E,\hbar m)(E,ℏm) form a set of points in the plane. By the Einstein–Brillouin–Keller rule above, near any regular value the points are the image of the lattice ℏ(n+12,m)\hbar(n+\tfrac12,m)ℏ(n+21​,m) (a libration in the polar angle, a rotation in the azimuth) under the smooth map (I1,I2)↦(E,Jz)(I_1,I_2)\mapsto(E,J_z)(I1​,I2​)↦(E,Jz​) inverse to the action map, asymptotically as ℏ→0\hbar\to0ℏ→0: locally a deformed square lattice.
