Lecture 9

The open Toda chain: integrability proved

What this lecture does

Everything in Lectures 1 to 8, applied to one nonlinear many-body system. We prove that the open Toda chain of NNN particles has NNN integrals, independent at every point and in involution: the definition of Lecture 4 is met in full. Then we check the hypotheses of the Liouville–Arnold theorem one by one, and one of them fails: the level sets are not compact, and they are not tori. What replaces the torus is scattering. The particles fly apart, their final momenta are the eigenvalues of the Lax matrix, and a three-body collision is a sequence of two-body ones — the classical fact that Lecture 16 quantises.

9.1 FPUT, and the Toda potential

In 1955 Fermi, Pasta, Ulam and Tsingou put a chain of masses joined by weakly nonlinear springs on a computer, with spring energy VFPUT(r)=r22+αr33, V_{\mathrm{FPUT}}(r)=\frac{r^{2}}2+\alpha\frac{r^{3}}3 , VFPUT​(r)=2r2​+α3r3​,(9.1) rrr the extension of the spring. They started it with all the energy in the lowest normal mode and expected the nonlinearity to share it out among all the modes. It did not: the energy wandered into a few neighbouring modes and came back, almost completely (Figure 1). A system that behaves this regularly is behaving as if it had more integrals than energy.

In 1967 Toda looked for a spring law for which the chain has exact periodic nonlinear waves, trying elliptic functions as the nonlinear analogue of sines, and found the exponential (Toda, §2.1). With the constants set to one and rn=qn+1−qnr_n=q_{n+1}-q_nrn​=qn+1​−qn​, VToda(r)=e−r+r−1=r22−r36+r424−⋯ , V_{\mathrm{Toda}}(r)=e^{-r}+r-1 =\frac{r^{2}}2-\frac{r^{3}}6+\frac{r^{4}}{24}-\cdots , VToda​(r)=e−r+r−1=2r2​−6r3​+24r4​−⋯,(9.2) which to cubic order is the FPUT spring, with α=−12\alpha=-\tfrac12α=−21​ (Figure 9.1). Compressed (r<0r<0r<0) it is a steep exponential wall; stretched, it softens to the constant force of the linear term.

For a ring of springs the linear terms add up to ∑nrn=\sum_nr_n=∑n​rn​= the circumference, a constant, and can be dropped; the result is the periodic chain of Lecture 10, whose Poincaré section is Figure 6.9. For an open chain nothing holds the ends, and we drop the linear terms by fiat.1 What is left is the Hamiltonian of Lecture 7, H=12∑n=1Npn2+∑n=1N−1eqn−qn+1, H=\frac12\sum_{n=1}^{N}p_n^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}e^{q_n-q_{n+1}}, H=21​n=1∑N​pn2​+n=1∑N−1​eqn​−qn+1​,(9.3) NNN unit masses on a line, each repelled by its neighbours (Figure 9.2). The repulsion is felt only when particle nnn comes close to, or overtakes, particle n+1n+1n+1. So the open chain has no FPUT oscillations: its particles fly apart. It is a nonlinear many-body system simple enough to take apart completely.

The torus conclusion of Theorem 4.8 is not claimed for the open chain, and it is false. §9.8 shows that k=0k=0k=0: every fibre is a copy of RN\R^{N}RN, with no periodic direction at all. If this feels like a let-down after five lectures of tori, it is the point. The definition of integrability is algebraic (Definition 4.6); the torus is a theorem, with a hypothesis, and here the hypothesis fails. What the open chain does on its non-compact fibres is the subject of the rest of this lecture: it scatters.

9.7 General NNN: elastic, non-diffractive scattering

For NNN particles the general solution has determinant formulas (Moser; Babelon–Bernard–Talon, §4.8), which we omit: the asymptotics are all we need.

Two words describe this (Figure 9.4). The scattering is elastic: the set of outgoing momenta equals the set of incoming ones, so each particle’s kinetic energy is handed on intact rather than shared. And it is non-diffractive: the outgoing momenta are not merely constrained by energy and momentum, which for N≥3N\ge3N≥3 would allow a continuum of outcomes; they are exactly the incoming ones, permuted. NNN conserved eigenvalues leave room for nothing else.

Three particles: collisions in either order

Look at (9.16) term by term. The line carrying λk\lambda_kλk​ picks up +2ln⁡(λj−λk)+2\ln(\lambda_j-\lambda_k)+2ln(λj​−λk​) from every faster momentum and −2ln⁡(λk−λj)-2\ln(\lambda_k-\lambda_j)−2ln(λk​−λj​) from every slower one: exactly the two-body displacements of Proposition 9.8, one per pair, added. The total depends on the eigenvalues only. In particular it does not depend on the order in which the pairs met.

Figure 9.5 makes it concrete. With incoming momenta 3,0,−33,0,-33,0,−3 on particles 1,2,31,2,31,2,3, the collisions exchange momenta pairwise until the order is reversed. It takes three exchanges, and they can happen in the order (12),(23),(12)(12),(23),(12)(12),(23),(12) or (23),(12),(23)(23),(12),(23)(23),(12),(23). The two runs start from different incoming lines and look different throughout, but each outgoing line is displaced from its own incoming line by the same amount in both. Written as permutations of the three momentum lines, s1s2s1=s2s1s2s_1s_2s_1=s_2s_1s_2s1​s2​s1​=s2​s1​s2​ (Figure 9.6), and the displacements respect it.

This is the concrete content of the phrase factorised scattering: the NNN-body scattering map is a product of two-body maps, and the product does not depend on how it is bracketed in time. The general theorem, that every NNN-body Toda collision decomposes this way, is Moser’s cited; what we have seen is the two-body map in closed form and the three-body composition checked. Lecture 16 quantises exactly this: the two-body displacement becomes a two-body S-matrix, and the braid relation becomes the Yang–Baxter equation.

9.8 Why the fibre is non-compact

Corollary 9.7 left the number kkk of periodic directions open. The scattering picture settles it.

Proposition 9.10 (The fibres are planes sketched). In Corollary 9.7, k=0k=0k=0: every Σ\SigmaΣ is diffeomorphic to RN\R^{N}RN.

A stabiliser element t≠0t\ne0t=0 means that the flow Φ\PhiΦ of G=∑ktkFkG=\sum_kt_kF_kG=∑k​tk​Fk​ returns every point of Σ\SigmaΣ to itself at time 111; in particular it returns the points x(t)x(t)x(t) of a trajectory at late times. On a torus the commuting flows have a full lattice of periods, even though a single irrational flow need never return (Figure 4.2). On the open chain’s fibre no combination of the flows ever returns; the proof used the physical flow of HHH, whose late-time configurations are free particles moving apart. In effect Σ≅RN\Sigma\cong\R^{N}Σ≅RN is coordinatised by the incoming offsets βn−\beta_n^{-}βn−​ of Theorem 9.9, while the asymptotic momenta are fixed on it (Figure 9.7). They are the integrals, and they are also the obstruction: as long as they are distinct, the particles separate.

Close the chain into a ring and this changes. The whole ring can still drift (the flow of F1F_1F1​ is still a translation), but the particles can no longer separate. Once the total momentum is fixed and the common translation split off, the relative level sets are compact, and each regular connected component is a torus TN−1T^{N-1}TN−1, whose actions are read off a curve. That is Lecture 10.

Summary

  • The model: the Toda spring e−r+r−1e^{-r}+r-1e−r+r−1 is FPUT’s to cubic order; the open chain (9.3) is pure nearest-neighbour repulsion, with integrals Fk=tr⁡LkF_k=\tr L^{k}Fk​=trLk, k=1,…,Nk=1,\dots,Nk=1,…,N.
  • Independence everywhere (Theorem 9.3): gradients of traces, the band lemma, and the Vandermonde determinant of the distinct eigenvalues.
  • Involution (Proposition 9.4): a constant skew r\rmatr with {L1,L2}=[r,L1+L2]\pb{\leg{L}{1}}{\leg{L}{2}}=\comm{\rmat}{\leg{L}{1}+\leg{L}{2}}{L1​,L2​}=[r,L1​+L2​]; it solves the modified CYBE, not the CYBE, and Jacobi holds anyway.
  • The audit (Theorem 9.5 and Corollary 9.7): Liouville integrable, flows complete, no fibre compact. Each fibre is RN\R^{N}RN (Proposition 9.10, sketched); the torus conclusion is false here.
  • Scattering: two particles exchange momenta, each line displaced by ∓2ln⁡(λ2−λ1)\mp2\ln(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)∓2ln(λ2​−λ1​) (Proposition 9.8). For NNN (Moser, cited) the asymptotic momenta are the eigenvalues of LLL, reversed; the scattering is elastic and non-diffractive, and the displacements add up pairwise, whatever the collision order.
