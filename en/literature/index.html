Lecture 0 · Why integrability?

Lecture 1 · Phase space, flows and Poisson brackets

Arnold, Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics, Ch. 8 (Hamiltonian vector fields, the Lie algebra of Hamiltonian functions, Liouville’s theorem); da Silva, Lectures on Symplectic Geometry, §§1–3 and 18 (manifolds, vector fields, Poisson structures); Babelon, Bernard and Talon, Introduction to Classical Integrable Systems, Ch. 1. Sign conventions differ across all three sources for precisely the identity of §1.5 — check any formula against (1.14) and (1.15) before transcribing it.

Lecture 2 · Differential forms

da Silva, Lectures on Symplectic Geometry, §6 (forms, d\ddd, the Poincaré lemma, Stokes); Arnold, Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics, §§32, 35–36 (exterior algebra, integration of forms, Stokes — and §44 for the action integral this lecture was built to support).

Lecture 3 · Symplectic manifolds

da Silva, Lectures on Symplectic Geometry, §§1–4 (symplectic forms, cotangent bundles and the tautological form, Lagrangian submanifolds, generating functions) and §§7–8 (the Moser trick and Darboux); Arnold, Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics, §§37–38 and 40–41 (symplectic structures, integral invariants, the Poisson bracket from ω\sympω), §43 (Darboux), §44 (the integral invariant of Poincaré–Cartan) and §§47–48 (Hamilton–Jacobi, generating functions); Bolsinov and Fomenko, Integrable Hamiltonian Systems, §1.3 (Darboux, and its version adapted to commuting functions). Conventions. da Silva’s agree with ours exactly — ω=−dα\symp=-\dd\alphaω=−dα with α\alphaα the tautological form, ιXHω=dH\iota_{\hvf{H}}\symp=\dd HιXH​​ω=dH, {f,g}=ω(Xf,Xg)\pb{f}{g}=\symp(\hvf{f},\hvf{g}){f,g}=ω(Xf​,Xg​), and the engine’s minus sign. Arnold and Bolsinov–Fomenko write ω=∑dp∧dq\symp=\sum\dd p\wedge\dd qω=∑dp∧dq, so their ω\sympω is minus ours: check any formula from them against (3.4) and Theorem 3.28 before transcribing it.

Lecture 4 · Liouville integrability and the Liouville–Arnold theorem

Arnold, Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics, §49 (the theorem and its proof; Lemma 2 of §49 C is our stage (iii), Lemma 3 of §49 D our Lemma 4.11) and §51 (conditionally periodic motion, averaging, resonances); Babelon, Bernard and Talon, Introduction to Classical Integrable Systems, ch. 2 (the theorem, and the examples); Cushman and Bates, Global Aspects of Classical Integrable Systems, ch. IV (the spherical pendulum: §3.4 the fibres, §5 monodromy); J. J. Duistermaat, “On global action-angle coordinates”, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 33 (1980); R. H. Cushman and J. J. Duistermaat, “The quantum mechanical spherical pendulum”, Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 19 (1988). Conventions. Arnold and Bolsinov–Fomenko write ω=∑dp∧dq\symp=\sum\dd p\wedge\dd qω=∑dp∧dq, the negative of ours; nothing in this lecture depends on the sign of ω\sympω, since every use of it is a statement that something vanishes.

Lecture 5 · Action-angle variables in practice

Arnold, Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics, §50 (action–angle variables: the construction and the neighbourhood statement) and §8 (central fields: §8 C the dense annulus, §8 D Bertrand’s theorem, through a sequence of problems); Babelon, Bernard and Talon, Introduction to Classical Integrable Systems, ch. 2; Dunajski, Integrable Systems, §1.2 (action–angle variables, and Kepler’s radial action by contour integration — a different route to Proposition 5.9); DLMF, §§19.2 and 19.12 (complete elliptic integrals and their behaviour as k→1k\to1k→1). Conventions. Arnold writes ω=∑dp∧dq\symp=\sum\dd p\wedge\dd qω=∑dp∧dq, the negative of ours, and so ω=d(p dq)\symp=\dd(p\,\dd q)ω=d(pdq) where we have −d(p⋅dq)-\dd(p\cdot\dd q)−d(p⋅dq); his actions come out positive for the same reason ours do (§3.7).

Lecture 6 · When integrability fails

Arnold, Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics, App. 8 (the perturbation problem, small denominators, Kolmogorov’s theorem with its hypotheses, §§A–D) and App. 9 (Poincaré’s geometric theorem); Kozlov, Integrability and non-integrability in Hamiltonian mechanics, Russian Math. Surveys 38:1 (1983), ch. IV §1 (Poincaré’s theorem, Theorems 1–2); Ott, Chaos in Dynamical Systems, 2nd ed., §7.2 (KAM, the Poincaré–Birkhoff alternation), §7.3 (the standard map), §4.4 (Lyapunov exponents); Toda, Theory of Nonlinear Lattices, §1.2 (Hénon–Heiles) and §2.9 (the three-particle chain and its reduction); Hénon and Heiles, Astron. J. 69 (1964) 73; H. Ito, Kodai Math. J. 8 (1985) 120, and J. J. Morales-Ruiz, J.-P. Ramis and C. Simó, Ann. Sci. ENS 40 (2007) 845, §6 (the Hénon–Heiles family). Conventions. Arnold’s App. 9 writes the symplectic form as dI∧dφ\dd I\wedge\dd\varphidI∧dφ, the negative of ours; areas and fixed points do not see the sign.

Lecture 7 · Lax pairs and isospectral flows
